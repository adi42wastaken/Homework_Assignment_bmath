\item  Letters in the Morse code are formed by a succession of dashes and dots with repetitions
permitted. How many letters is it possible to form with ten symbols or less?
 $$\sum_{k=1}^{10} 2^k=2^{11}-2$$
\item \textbf{Consider a grid with points $(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$. Suppose you  start at (0, 0) and at each step you can go either one step up or one step to the right. How many paths are there from (0, 0) to (100, 100)? How many paths from $(0, 0)$ to (100, 100) pass through (60, 40)?}\\
So we have to go right 100 times and up 100 times. So the number of paths is some combination of \begin{verbatim}
  r r r ... r u u u ... u
\end{verbatim}
This we obviously know is just $\binom{200}{100}.$\\
In second one, we first go to $(60,40)$ and then go from $(60,40)$ to $(100,100)$. So the number of paths is $\binom{100}{60}^2.$
\item If 8 identical blackboards are to be divided among 4 schools, how many divisions are possible?
$$\boxed{\binom{11}{3}}$$
How many if each school must receive at least 1 blackboard?
$$\boxed{\binom{7}{3}}$$
\item Suppose that 10 fish are caught at a lake that contains 5 distinct types of fish.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item How many different outcomes are possible, where an outcome specifies the numbers of
caught fish of each of the 5 types?
$$\boxed{\binom{14}{4}}$$
\item How many outcomes are possible when 3 of the 10 fish caught are trout?
$$\boxed{\binom{10}{3}}$$
\item How many when at least 2 of the 10 are trout?
$$\boxed{\binom{12}{4}}$$
\end{enumerate}
\item If $r_1$ indistinguishable things of one kind and $r_2$ indistinguishable things of a second kind are placed into $n$ cells, find the number of distinguishable arrangements.

\end{enumerate}
